This year, I had the chance to participate in the annual Cybersecurity Challenge Belgium, organized by NVISO. The event attracts students from academic institutions across Belgium, both bachelor’s and master’s, who have a focus on information technology.
The competition is a CTF (Capture the Flag) event, a popular type of cybersecurity contest where participants try to solve a variety of security-related challenges to find hidden pieces of information called “flags”. 
Each flag is proof that you’ve solved a challenge, and you submit it to earn points. Who has the most points wins! 
I teamed up with a fellow intern, and together we set out to solve as many challenges as possible over two days.

The challenges were divided into several categories:
- Cryptography
- Forensics
- Mobile
- Web
- Programming
- Reverse Engineering
- Miscellaneous (more like a series of riddles)

## Our Approach and Experience
We started off strong by answering all the theory-based cybersecurity questions, thanks to what we’d learned in our studies. Then we dove into the tougher, hands-on challenges. My teammate excelled at reverse engineering, while I found myself drawn to the miscellaneous tasks. These were unpredictable and often required a bit of creative Googling and thinking outside the box—they really tested both my technical skills and general knowledge.
One of the coolest moments was tracking down a manual from 1806 — [the Admiral Home Popham Telegraph Signal Book](https://3decks.pbworks.com/f/Admiral%2520Home%2520Popham%2520Telegraph%2520signal%2520book%2520Final%2520edition.pdf) — to decode a message written with actual flags. This signaling language was once used for communication between ships, and it was amazing to see it pop up in a modern CTF!

The message from the challenge trasnslate to:
> "England will attack us with at least 189798411 men"

## Final thoughts
Looking back, we definitely would have benefited from more preparation. CTF challenges have their own unique style and logic — they’re not the same as running a standard vulnerability assessment. As always the more you practice, the better you get.
