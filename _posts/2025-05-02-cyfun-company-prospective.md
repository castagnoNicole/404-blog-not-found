Another great Tech&Meet session at Howest!
This time, the topic was the CyberFundamentals Framework, and we had the privilege of learning from Johan Klykens, Director Competent Authority on Cybersecurity Certification for the CCB — and, luckily for us, a guest lecturer at Howest.

The event drew in a mix of students like me eager to go deeper into the framework, as well as researchers and both technical and non-technical representatives from Belgian companies.

## A Company Perspective on CyberFundamentals
What made this talk especially valuable was its focus on how companies can use the CyberFundamentals Framework in practice — something we hadn’t really covered in class. 
Johan Klykens explained not just the “what,” but the “how” and “why” behind the framework’s structure.

One line from Mr. Klykens really stuck with me:
> “Cyfun is not a checklist, it’s a map.”

He emphasized that the Cyfun requirements are interconnected. You can start by implementing the “basic” level requirements and then progressively build on them, aiming for the right cybersecurity level based on your company’s regulatory obligations and risk assessment.
Trying to implement every key requirements at once would be a tall order with unpredictable challeges.
To prioritize, he suggested focusing on key measures and getting a clear overview of your current situation. He also reminded us that exceptions are allowed, as long as they’re properly documented and justified.

## Final Thoughts
I’ve used the CyberFundamentals Framework both to prioritize my targets during pentests and to structure my findings and recommendations in reports. 
It’s the only framework I know of that’s been tested against real-life cyber attacks from the past ten years, which makes it incredibly practical and relevant.
